Pressure is a match state

A delivery is classified from the score, wickets, and legal balls remaining immediately before it. If multiple rules apply, it is still counted once.

01 / FINISH PHASE

30 balls or fewer remain

Captures the final five overs of either innings, when each scoring or stopping opportunity has immediate leverage.

02 / WICKET SQUEEZE

Five down, time short

At least five wickets have fallen, 48 legal balls have elapsed, and 72 or fewer remain.

03 / CHASE SQUEEZE

The asking rate bites

Second innings, after 18 legal balls: required rate is 9.5+, or 7.5+ and at least 2.5 above the current rate.

How the index is built

Only IPL and T20I competitions with a readable season from 2022 onward are included. A season label is read as its leading four-digit year, so a competition labelled 2022/23 belongs to season 2022. Super-over innings (innings 3 and 4) are skipped. The in-scope data contains 837 matches and 193,988 deliveries: 81,806 pressure (42.2%) and 112,182 ordinary. The supplied ball-by-ball file has 200,999 raw rows; the difference is excluded competitions, skipped super-over innings, or out-of-scope records, not missing rows. Players who represented Pakistan in T20Is in the supplied data are excluded from the primary lists. The source has no dedicated nationality field, so Pakistan T20I team representation is used as the nationality proxy; 53 player IDs are excluded. Five players with confirmed IPL playing retirements are also excluded: Kieron Pollard, Dwayne Bravo, Ambati Rayudu, Dinesh Karthik, and Ravichandran Ashwin. Mohit Sharma is separately excluded: the cited IPL.com article reports that he announced his retirement from all forms of cricket in December 2025 and is expected to become CSK's bowling coach for IPL 2027. International-only retirements and unconfirmed rumors are not used. The problem statement does not require any of these exclusions, so the lists without them are reported under "Sensitivity and exclusions". These exclusions are applied before scoring and format-season standardization. Players are analyzed by format and season. A player-format-season segment must have at least 60 pressure balls, 8 matches containing pressure batting/bowling, and 60 ordinary balls. After format-season roll-up, 60 batter profiles and 122 bowler profiles qualify; 11 players qualify in both disciplines for the all-rounder list. The dashboard also offers 36-ball/5-match and 90-ball/10-match sensitivity settings; their results are in "Sensitivity and exclusions".

Mohit Sharma's retirement and expected CSK coaching role: IPL.com.

Retirement evidence: Mumbai Indians on Pollard; ESPNcricinfo on Bravo; Cricbuzz on Rayudu; ESPNcricinfo on Karthik's final IPL season; News18 on Ashwin.

Batting inputs

Higher pressure strike rate, strike-rate improvement over the player's ordinary baseline, lower dismissal rate per 100 balls, and greater positive-match share.

Bowling inputs

Lower pressure economy, improvement over the player's ordinary economy, more pressure wickets per 100 legal balls, and greater positive-match share.

Weights and shrinkage

Each direction-aligned input is a z-score against eligible players in that format-season stratum.

0.35 × output + 0.30 × baseline change
+ 0.20 × outcome + 0.15 × match consistency

Reliability is √((balls/(balls+60)) × (matches/(matches+6))). Index = clip(50 + 10 × weighted score × reliability, 0, 100). Reliability is a heuristic shrinkage factor, not a confidence interval.

All-rounder method

All-rounders must qualify for both discipline lists under the same format, season, and sample filters. Composite index = 0.50 × batting index + 0.50 × bowling index. Each component already includes its own reliability adjustment. This is a dual-discipline performance ranking, not an official player-role label.

Consistency, data checks, and limits

  • A batter pressure match is positive when (pressure SR − usual SR) − 2 × (pressure outs/ball − usual outs/ball) > 0. A bowler match is positive when (usual economy − pressure economy) + 1.5 × (pressure wickets/100 balls − usual wickets/100 balls) > 0.
  • Run-outs, retired hurt/out (including the source spelling Retire Out), timed out, handled-the-ball, and obstructing-the-field dismissals are not bowler wickets. A non-striker run-out contributes to that batter's dismissal rate but not balls faced.
  • The extras fields are inconsistent: extras1_runs is 6 on ordinary deliveries and has categorical-looking values on tagged extras. The analysis does not treat it as runs. One-run penalties are added for wides, no-balls and the undocumented other type (264 rows, treated as a no-ball); bye/leg-bye totals are unavailable and omitted, so reconstructed chase rates are approximate.
  • The supplied boundary flags disagree with runs on 23 deliveries; batter runs use runs, not the flags. Revised-overs values above 20 are legal-ball counts (114 means 19 overs). Revised targets/overs are used when present, but full Duckworth-Lewis par scores and ball-level win probability are not available.
  • Pressure thresholds are transparent proxies, not direct measures of psychology; they may miss rivalry, knockout stakes, crowd conditions, or a close first-innings situation. Rankings are not causal estimates.
  • Small T20I pools: with exclusions on and the default thresholds, T20I format-season pools hold 5 to 10 eligible batters and 12 to 36 eligible bowlers, against 12 to 17 batters and 31 to 36 bowlers in IPL seasons. A z-score inside a pool of five to ten players moves a lot when one player is added or removed. A plausible consequence is that players whose ranking rests mainly on T20I seasons are ranked higher or lower than a larger pool would give them. This is a hypothesis; its size has not been measured.
  • Season labels: competitions labelled 2022/23, 2023/24 and 2024/25 (18 matches) are assigned to their first calendar year. Series that run across two calendar years are pooled with that year's other competitions in the format-season z-scores. An earlier version of this analysis dropped these 18 matches; the "Before fixes" rows in the sensitivity table show the effect.
  • The other extras type (264 rows) is undocumented. Treating it as a no-ball, with a one-run penalty charged to the bowler, is an inference from the data, not from documentation. The penalty run matters for the batter list: it adds 70 pressure deliveries, which lifts Cooper Connolly over the 60-ball / 8-match threshold. A separate Python re-implementation (not the shipped code) that treated other as an illegal ball without the penalty run kept Sai Sudharsan and Nitish Reddy in the batter top ten and left Connolly out.
  • Super overs: innings 3 and 4 (38 rows in six matches, 26 rows in the four matches that are in scope) are skipped. Their balls are neither pressure nor ordinary. Treating every super-over ball as pressure would be a defensible alternative and was not applied.
  • The player exclusions are a selection decision, not a feature of the method. Every list that shows the effect of removing them is in "Sensitivity and exclusions".
Reading the leaders: Rishabh Pant leads the batter list at 58.4, with 217 pressure balls in 17 matches, a 194.9 pressure strike rate versus 123.5 ordinarily, and a positive result in 82% of pressure matches. Sanju Samson and Cooper Connolly are within 0.2 index points of him. Suryakumar Yadav has the largest pressure sample in the batter top ten: 724 balls and 90% reliability. Akash Madhwal leads the bowling index at 61.8, with a 7.35 pressure economy against 10.50 ordinarily over 93 balls in eight matches. Sunil Narine and Umran Malik follow at 61.3 each, and Narine ranks higher on pressure balls. Umran Malik's 6.43 pressure economy against 10.06 ordinarily comes from 97 balls in nine matches. The index therefore distinguishes rate performance from sample certainty.

Reproduction

Use Node.js 20 LTS (package.json declares Node 18 or newer; the committed analysis_data.json was generated with Node 20). From the data folder, with ball_by_ball.csv, matches.csv and competitions.csv present, run node analyze.mjs to regenerate analysis_data.json. The dashboard and ranked lists use scoring.mjs. Run node server.mjs and open http://127.0.0.1:4173. Filters recalculate rankings. Export rankings downloads one CSV with the current top ten batters, bowlers, and all-rounders for the selected filters.
